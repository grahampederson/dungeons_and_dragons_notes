**Introducing Naia — "The Leak"**

*A good jumping-off point for a newly formed party: a crossroads tavern, inn, or waystation — somewhere with a bit of plumbing, a roof, and strangers who don't know each other yet.*

---

The party's gathered wherever new parties gather — a common room, a job board, a fire pit at a waystation, whatever your DM's using as the opening scene. There's the usual noise: mugs, arguments, someone trying to recruit help for a job nobody's fully explained yet.

And underneath all of it, quiet and easy to miss at first, there's a sound. *Tok... tok-tok... tok...* Irregular. Coming from somewhere near the back wall.

If anyone actually looks, they'll spot her: a woman with skin that seems to catch and hold the firelight strangely, faint ripples of color moving under it like light through shallow water, crouched by a rain barrel or a cracked section of piping, head tilted, completely absorbed. She's got a battered, water-stained notebook open on her knee, sketching something — not the room, not a person, just a small diagram of overlapping circles.

She's not part of the job. She's not part of the conversation. She wandered in an hour ago, ordered nothing, and got distracted by a leak.

**The hook:** Whoever's recruiting for the job — an NPC, a job board, whatever your table's using — either notices her sitting there and, out of options or curiosity, asks if she's looking for work. Or one of the new party members, mid-argument about the job, gets fed up and snaps something like "does anyone else want to weigh in, or is the monk just going to keep fixing my landlord's gutter?"

Naia looks up like she'd genuinely forgotten other people were in the room.

**Suggested first lines, adapt as needed:**

*"That drip's uneven because the seam's warped, not because the barrel's cracked — someone patched it wrong. ...Sorry. You were saying something about a job?"*

She'll answer questions plainly, without much flourish — no dramatic monologue about her past, no immediate offer of her tragic backstory. If asked what she's doing here, in this town, at this crossroads, she'll say something honest but incomplete: *"Following something. Not sure yet if it's a person or just a feeling."* — which is true, and also the closest she'll get to mentioning her parents on day one.

She's polite, a little too literal, and clearly comfortable with silence in a way that makes chattier characters fill the gap. If someone in the party is visibly anxious, hot-headed, or spiraling about the job ahead, she might offer one calm, slightly clinical observation — *"Your breathing's shallow. That'll slow your sword arm before it slows your nerve."* — delivered without judgment, more like a passing diagnosis than advice.

She joins the job for practical reasons she'll state plainly if asked — needs the coin, needs the direction, hasn't had a reason to move in a while, this one's as good as any. The real reason (restlessness, grief, an unresolved wound she's finally decided to walk toward) she keeps to herself. That's for the party to earn later.

**Calming things she could say**  
*"Your breathing's shallow. That'll slow your arm before it slows your nerve."*  
*"You're gripping that hilt like it's going to run. It's not going anywhere. Neither are you, yet."*  
*"Whatever you're about to say — say it after your shoulders drop. It'll land better."*  
*"Panic's just a decision you haven't noticed you're making yet. You can unmake it."*  
*"I've watched water do exactly this — churn, go loud, go nowhere. Then it finds a line through. You will too."*  
*"You don't need to be calm. You need to be still enough to see straight. Different thing."*  
*"Count the drips." (gestures vaguely at nothing in particular, dead serious) "There's always something dripping somewhere. Focus on it a moment."*  
*"That's fear talking faster than the situation actually requires."*  
*"Whatever's coming, it's coming at the same speed either way. Shouting won't hurry it or slow it down — might as well save the breath."*  
*"You're not wrong to be afraid. You're just early. Save it for when it's useful."*

You don't need to write a novel. A few paragraphs, bullet points, or even simple answers are perfectly fine. The goal is to help bring your character to life and give the DM things they can use throughout the campaign.

1\. Where does your character feel like they belong?

**Home: The Cistern Monastery, at the edge of the Salt Marsh of Vell**

Naia wasn't born there — genasi rarely are born anywhere near where they end up. She was born to a trading family who worked the coastal barges, and for years "home" was just wherever the boat tied up that night. But when she was eleven, a storm took the family boat and her parents with it. She washed up — half-drowned, water genasi or not — near a monastery built into the ruins of an old Roman-style cistern, half-submerged in the marsh.

**The people:** Master Ithren, the monastery's eldest teacher, is the one who gave Naia her name — her parents' names were lost with the boat, and rather than mourn a word she couldn't remember, Ithren let her choose one. He's blunt, unsentimental, and has never once told her she's talented — but he's also never let another student spar with her without saying "watch her feet" first, which she's learned is his version of pride.

Then there's Coen, a fellow initiate who's the opposite of meticulous — loud, clumsy, cheerfully bad at stillness meditation. He's the reason Naia learned to laugh again instead of just perform calm. She misses arguing with him about nothing.

**The place itself:** The cistern floods and drains with the marsh tides, so the whole monastery breathes with the water — meditation halls half-underwater at dawn, dry stone by dusk. Naia learned her stances on floors that were sometimes solid ground and sometimes knee-deep water, which is probably why she fights just as well in either.

**What she misses:** The particular quiet of the hall right after the tide recedes — that half-hour where the stone is still dripping, still settling, and nobody talks. She associates that silence with being safe for the first time after the storm.

**Bonus — the small ordinary thing:** A specific *sound*, not an object — the low, hollow *tok...tok...tok* of water dripping from the cistern ceiling into the collection basins at uneven intervals. Every monastery in the world has dripping water. But that rhythm — irregular, unhurried, patient — is unmistakably *hers*. She'd know it blind, in a cave, underwater, anywhere.

2\. What made you into the person you are?

**What made her: The night the cistern flooded wrong.**

When Naia was sixteen, the marsh had the worst storm surge in a generation — worse than the one that killed her parents, though she didn't let herself think about that at the time. The monastery's tidal rhythm, the thing she'd built her entire sense of safety around, broke. Water came up through the meditation halls fast and high, faster than the monks' calm, controlled breathing exercises had ever prepared anyone for.

Ithren gave the order to evacuate to the upper stone. Coen didn't make it out in time — got pinned when a support beam gave way in the flooding lower hall. Naia went back in.

She got him out. But it wasn't stillness that did it — it was the opposite. She smashed through debris, half-panicked, moving faster and messier than anything Open Hand training had ever taught her. Afterward, soaked and shaking on the upper stone with Coen coughing beside her, Ithren said the only thing he ever said that sounded like real praise: "That wasn't your training. That was you."

It cracked something open in her. She'd spent five years since her parents' death believing that calm was the same thing as safety — that if she could just get still enough, controlled enough, nothing could take anyone from her again. That night taught her calm has limits, and sometimes the thing that saves someone isn't discipline, it's *refusing to let go*.

**Her strong belief — possibly wrong:** *Stillness is a choice you make, not a state you're given.* Naia genuinely believes anyone can learn to be calm under pressure, that panic is just insufficiently practiced discipline, and that people who fall apart in a crisis simply haven't done the work yet.

It's not true, or at least not fully — some people's nervous systems just don't work that way, trauma doesn't respond evenly to repetition, and she's arguably only "proof" of her own theory because she happened to have five years of intensive training *and* a monastery full of people who caught her when she broke. But she doesn't see the scaffolding under her own composure. She sees discipline. This makes her occasionally impatient — even a little judgmental — toward party members who spiral, freeze, or lose their nerve, before she catches herself and remembers Coen, pinned under a beam, and how her own stillness didn't save him either.

3\. What makes your character happy?

**What makes Naia happy: Fixing things that *drip wrong*.**

It sounds absurd, but it traces straight back to that sound she misses — the *tok...tok...tok* of the cistern's collection basins. Somewhere along the way, she started noticing when water moved *badly*: a gutter with an uneven splash, a leaking waterskin, a fountain that gurgled instead of flowed clean. And she can't leave it alone. She'll crouch by a tavern's broken rain barrel for twenty minutes adjusting the angle of a downspout while the rest of the party waits, just because the drip was landing wrong.

It's not really about the water. It's about restoring a rhythm — making something chaotic into something predictable again. Master Ithren would probably say it's the same instinct that made her a monk in the first place, just aimed at buckets instead of herself.

**For fun, off-duty:** She sketches. Not landscapes or people — *patterns*. Ripple diagrams, the way water moves around obstacles, the geometry of waves hitting a wall at different angles. She's got a battered, water-stained notebook she's carried since the monastery, filled with these sketches. It's half art, half genuine obsessive study, and she'd be mildly embarrassed if anyone paged through it and realized how many pages are just... circles.

**Two weeks with nothing to do:** She wouldn't rest, not exactly — Naia doesn't really believe in idle rest, that's too close to the stillness-that-isn't-earned she distrusts. Instead she'd find a body of water — a lake, a slow river, anything — and spend the two weeks basically re-training there. Long swims. Forms practiced in shallows and drop-offs. Probably one full day just sitting at the edge of it doing nothing but watching how it moves, filling half her notebook. To an outside observer that would look suspiciously like a vacation. She would insist it's not.

**Campaign-usable quirk:** She physically can't ignore a bad drip, leak, or misaligned water feature. If the party's in a dungeon with a slow ceiling leak, an inn with a broken pipe, or a fountain that sounds "off," Naia will drift toward it mid-conversation and start fiddling. It's a nice beat for DMs to hang small clues or moments of levity on — and a good running bit for the table ("Naia, we're being ambushed—" "One second, this drainage is criminal").

4\. Who are the important people in their life?

**1\. Coen Ashgrove — Someone she loves/deeply cares about**

*Relationship: Found-family, closest friend since childhood at the monastery.*

Coen is everything Naia's discipline isn't — loud, clumsy, incapable of sitting still through a meditation session without cracking a joke halfway through. He never took to Open Hand training the way she did; he's still a middling fighter at best, more likely to trip over his own stance than land a clean strike. But he's fiercely loyal and has an uncanny gift for making people laugh at exactly the moment they need to stop taking themselves seriously — including Naia, especially Naia.

Their history is the flood: Naia went back into the collapsing cistern hall to pull him out from under a support beam, and it's the event that cracked open her whole worldview about stillness and control. But it goes both ways — Coen never lets her forget that *she* wasn't calm that night, she was terrified and desperate, and he uses it (gently, constantly) to needle her out of her more self-serious moments. He's the person who knew her before she became "the disciplined one," and stayed anyway. If he's not traveling with her, she carries his name like a compass point — one of the people she measures decisions against.

---

**2\. Master Ithren — Someone who pushes her to become better**

*Relationship: Mentor, the monk who found and raised her at the Cistern Monastery.*

Ithren is unsentimental almost to the point of coldness — decades of discipline sanded off anything that looked like softness, or so it seems. He rarely praises directly; his highest compliment to a student is silence followed by a slightly longer sparring session. He named Naia when she had no name left to give, but he's never once treated that as owing her gentleness — if anything, he's harder on her than on other students, because he clocked her potential early and refuses to let her coast on natural talent.

The defining moment between them came *after* the flood, not during it — when he told her "that wasn't your training, that was you," which was simultaneously the kindest and most destabilizing thing he'd ever said to her. It planted a question she still hasn't resolved: if her best moment wasn't her discipline, what exactly has she spent ten years building? He's not currently traveling with her, but she still frames hard decisions as "what would Ithren say" — usually right before deciding to do the opposite, just to see if she's actually grown past needing his approval.

---

**3\. Sera Voss — Someone she has a history of conflict with**

*Relationship: Former fellow initiate at the monastery, now a rival adventurer/mercenary.*

Sera trained alongside Naia and Coen for years — talented, ambitious, and openly contemptuous of what she saw as the monastery's "wasted" isolation. Where Naia found safety in the cistern's walls, Sera found them suffocating. She left five years before Naia did, publicly breaking with Ithren over a philosophical dispute: Sera believed the monks' pursuit of inner stillness was really just fear of the world dressed up as virtue, and she said so, loudly, on her way out the door.

Naia took it personally, in a way she still hasn't fully admitted — because a part of her privately agrees with pieces of Sera's argument, especially after the flood. They've crossed paths twice since as adventurers, both times circling the same job or the same reward, and both times it ended in a genuinely nasty argument that had nothing to do with the actual work. Sera isn't a villain — she's sharp, capable, and not entirely wrong about Naia's blind spots, which is exactly what makes her so hard to be around. Naia would rather fight a monster than sit across a table from Sera Voss.

5\. Why did they become an adventurer?

**Why she learned the class:**

Naia learned Open Hand technique the way every initiate at the Cistern Monastery does — starting the morning after she was pulled from the marsh, half by accident. Ithren didn't sit her down and announce she'd be trained; he just started including her in the dawn forms because it gave a traumatized eleven-year-old somewhere to put her hands and her attention besides grief. The training wasn't originally *for* anything. It was closer to physical therapy that happened to also be a martial art.

The relationship with Ithren as a teacher was exacting, not warm — he corrected her stance a thousand times before he ever asked how she was doing, and even then only obliquely, disguised as tactical questions ("Where's your weight? No — where is it *actually*.") It took her years to understand that was his form of care. She trusts precision because precision is the language he loved her in.

**What finally made her leave:**

It wasn't the flood — that happened five years before she left, and if anything it made her cling to the monastery harder. What actually pushed her out the door was smaller and stranger: about a year after the flood, a traveling merchant came through the marsh selling salvage from wrecked boats, and among it was a barge nameplate with weathering and paint that matched her family's boat. Not proof. Not even close to proof. But close enough that she spent three sleepless nights turning it over, and close enough that she realized something uncomfortable — she'd built ten years of stillness on top of a wound she'd never actually looked at directly. She knew almost nothing about how her parents died, where the wreck settled, whether there was anything left to find.

Ithren, to his credit, didn't try to stop her. He just told her the marsh had taught her everything it could about controlling water. It hadn't taught her anything about the ocean that took her parents, and she wouldn't learn that sitting still.

**What she's hoping for:**

On the surface, it's about the past — she wants to find out what actually happened the night her family's boat went down, maybe recover something, maybe just stand on the water it happened in and finally know instead of wonder. That's the mission she'd say out loud if you asked her.

Underneath that, though, she's testing her own belief system in the field — the one from that flood night, that stillness is a choice and discipline can be built strong enough to hold against anything. Every dangerous, chaotic, uncontrolled situation adventuring throws at her is, whether she admits it or not, a chance to find the edges of that belief. She's hoping to prove Ithren's training was enough. She's a little afraid of finding out where it isn't.

6\. What are your character's weaknesses?

**Core flaw: She mistakes control for safety, and she'll sacrifice almost anything to keep the illusion of control intact.**

We've already seeded this — her belief that "stillness is a choice, not a state you're given" — but the flip side is uglier: when situations spiral genuinely out of her control, Naia doesn't calmly adapt. She either freezes for a beat too long (because her whole toolkit is built for controllable chaos, not true chaos) or she overcorrects violently, trying to force order onto something that can't be forced. Either way, it's a gap between how she sees herself and how she actually operates under real pressure — and it's the gap Sera Voss loves to point at.

**Bad at, despite being otherwise talented:** She's a poor liar and worse at reading deception in others. Monastic life trained her to read *bodies* — stance, breath, weight distribution — but not faces, not social misdirection, not the specific human skill of saying one thing while meaning another. She'll clock that someone's *physically* tense in a negotiation but completely miss that they're bluffing about the price. Party members who are good talkers should probably take point on cons and negotiations; Naia's the one who gets suckered by a smooth story.

---

**Break the law:** If she found solid evidence about her parents' wreck — say, salvage rights held by a noble or merchant house that's sitting on wreckage or records and refusing access — she would absolutely break into a private warehouse or bribe/threaten a records-keeper to get at it. She'd tell herself it's proportionate. It wouldn't be.

**Pick a fight she shouldn't:** Anyone repeating Sera's old accusation — that the monastery's calm was really just fear in disguise — hits a nerve Naia hasn't fully dealt with. Said by the wrong person at the wrong moment (drunk mercenary, smug noble, doesn't matter), she'll escalate a war of words into something physical faster than her training should allow, and she'll know she's doing it, and do it anyway.

**Risk her life:** Any drowning, flooding, or trapped-underwater scenario. It's not just heroism — it's Coen's beam collapse, replaying. She will go back into a collapsing, flooding structure for a stranger with roughly the same reckless commitment she once had for Coen, because some part of her is still trying to win that night a second time.

**Betray someone:** If Ithren ever asked her to choose between the monastery's secrecy/safety and a party member's mission — say, the party needed information the monks hold sacred or protected — she might genuinely choose the monastery. Not out of malice, but because that debt runs deeper than most relationships she's built since, and she hasn't fully reckoned with how much authority he still holds over her.

**Emotionally-driven decision:** Finding out Sera Voss is in real danger — even mid-argument, even mid-rivalry — and abandoning a smarter tactical plan to go pull her out anyway, muttering the whole time about how she's an idiot for doing it.

**Impossible risk for someone she cares about:** Diving into open ocean — genuinely open water, not the marsh she's comfortable in, the specific thing that killed her parents — if it meant getting to Coen, or increasingly, a party member who's earned that place in her list. It would cost her every ounce of composure she's built her identity around, and she'd do it without pausing to calculate the odds.

7\. What would truly matter to your character?

**What she wants, underneath everything: to know, once, without having to hold herself together to survive knowing.**

Not the answer about her parents specifically — she'll probably get some version of that answer eventually, the campaign will likely give it to her. What she actually wants is bigger and quieter than that: she wants to reach the end of her life having felt one real, uncontained thing — grief, joy, terror, doesn't matter which — without immediately converting it into breath-work and stillness and *managing* it. Every emotional experience she's had since age eleven has been filtered through the question "how do I stay composed through this," and some buried part of her suspects she's never actually let herself finish grieving her parents, or fully feel what almost losing Coen meant, because she was always simultaneously performing the *processing* of it.

If you asked her directly what she wants from life, she'd probably say something training-shaped: mastery, a return to genuine stillness, maybe eventually training students of her own the way Ithren trained her. That's the answer she believes.

But the real marker of a life worth living, if she ever let herself say it out loud, would be smaller and far more frightening to her: *someone who saw her fall apart completely — not composed, not disciplined, not managing it — and stayed anyway.* Not because she needed rescuing. Just stayed, the way she stayed for Coen, the way Ithren never quite did outside of training. Proof that she doesn't have to be controlled to be worth loving.

She'd never phrase it that way. She'd say she wants to find peace, or closure, or to honor her parents' memory. But the thing underneath the thing — the one she's spent a decade of discipline avoiding — is that she wants to know what happens if she just *lets go*, once, and finds out the people around her don't leave.

That's probably the emotional climax waiting for her somewhere in this campaign: a moment where holding it together isn't an option, and she has to discover whether her worldview survives being genuinely, uncontrollably human.

Then answer:

**What she's afraid she'll never get to experience: an ordinary day that doesn't require earning it.**

Somewhere underneath the mission, the discipline, the whole architecture of who she's built herself into, is a much smaller and more embarrassing fear — that she'll never get to have a day that's just *boring*. A morning where nothing needs managing, no grief needs processing, no skill needs sharpening, and she isn't quietly auditing herself for signs of cracking. Every day since the storm that took her parents has felt like something she had to survive, get through, or prove herself worthy of — first grief, then training, then the flood, then the road. She doesn't actually know what she'd do with a day that asked nothing of her. She suspects, secretly, that she wouldn't know how to be a person in it. That the stillness she's built is only ever load-bearing, never restful — and if she ever set the weight down, there might not be anyone underneath it.

Tied to that: she's afraid she'll never get to grow old with people who knew her *before* — that Coen will eventually settle somewhere, that Ithren will die at the monastery, that the handful of relationships that predate her armor will all end before she figures out how to be known by anyone the way they knew her. She's terrified, essentially, that composure is the only self she'll ever get to offer anyone, permanently, and that whatever ordinary, unguarded version of Naia might exist underneath it will simply never get the conditions to show up.

She would never say any of this to the party. If asked what she's afraid of, she'd deflect toward something tactical — heights, maybe, or losing a fight that matters. This one stays buried. It's the kind of thing that would only come out by accident, in a quiet moment, probably to the one person who's earned enough trust to catch her off guard — which, mechanically, is a great thread for a DM or a fellow player to eventually pull.

Building the character

Here's a build that backs up everything we've established — precision, water affinity, emotional control, and that "reads bodies not faces" flaw.

### **Ability Scores**

**Priority: DEX \> WIS \> CON \> everything else**

| Stat | Suggested (Point Buy/Standard Array) | Why |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| DEX | 15-16 (+ Genasi racial bonus if applicable) | AC, attack rolls, initiative — monk's core stat |
| WIS | 14-15 | AC (monk unarmored defense), Ki save DC, and thematically her "read the room/water" instinct |
| CON | 13-14 | HP and Con saves (concentration isn't relevant to monk, but survivability matters) |
| STR | 8-10 | Dump stat — she's precision, not power |
| INT | 8-10 | Dump stat, ironically, despite her being "meticulous" — that's INT-flavored but not INT-mechanical |
| CHA | 8-10 | Dump stat — reinforces her "bad liar, bad at reading deception" flaw perfectly |

*(Water Genasi in most editions get \+2 CON, \+1 WIS — check whichever ruleset/sourcebook your table uses, since ASI structure varies between 2014 and 2024 rules.)*

### **Race: Water Genasi traits to lean into**

* **Acid resistance** and **swim speed** — mechanically reinforces the "comfortable in water/marsh" backstory  
* **Unending Breath** (can hold breath indefinitely) — huge thematically for the ocean/drowning fear arc  
* **Call to the Wave / minor water-shaping cantrip-like ability** (varies by source) — great flavor for "fixing drips," could even be reflavored as her fidgeting with leaky pipes

### **Class: Monk, Way of the Open Hand**

**Key skill proficiencies to take:**

* **Acrobatics** — DEX-based, monk staple, fits her physical precision  
* **Insight** — WIS-based; this is important. Even though she's *bad at reading deception in faces*, Insight (reading bodies/tension/stance) is exactly her stated strength. Great mechanical expression of "reads bodies, not faces."  
* **Athletics** or **Survival** — either fits the marsh/monastery upbringing and swimming

**Skills to deliberately NOT prioritize (fits her flaw):**

* **Deception** and **Persuasion** — leave these low. If your table uses backgrounds/skill choice, actively steer away from these so the mechanical build matches "poor liar, easily fooled by smooth talkers"

### **Background: Hermit (fits better than you'd expect)**

Even though she wasn't a solitary hermit exactly, the **Hermit background** gives you Medicine \+ Religion (or swap Religion for something else with DM approval), a discovery/secret hook (perfect for the parents' wreck mystery), and mechanically reflects a decade of monastery isolation. Alternatively, **Acolyte** works if you want to emphasize the monastic/spiritual training angle more than the isolation angle.

### **Subclass features that reinforce backstory (Way of Open Hand)**

* **Open Hand Technique** — her strikes can knock prone, push, or deny reactions; this is the "precision fighter" flavor, controlled and deliberate rather than wild  
* **Wholeness of Body** (level 6\) — self-healing that fits her identity as someone who's spent her life learning to hold herself together, literally  
* **Tranquility** (level 11\) — grants sanctuary-like effect during rest; thematically perfect, since it's *literally* her worldview (stillness as protection) made mechanical

### **Feat/ASI suggestions**

* **Fey Touched or Metamagic Adept** — not thematically necessary  
* **Better fits: Martial Adept** or, more thematically, **Telekinetic** (subtle nudges — could reflavor as micro water manipulation) or just straight ASI into WIS/DEX for a tighter, more classically effective monk  
* If your table allows multiclass dips: a **single level of Fighter (or nothing)** isn't necessary — Naia's concept is stronger as a pure monk. Resist the dip; discipline is kind of the whole point.

### **Saving throw proficiencies (fixed by class)**

Monks get **STR and DEX** saves — slightly ironic since her emotional throughline is about *resisting* impulsive/uncontrolled reactions, but DEX saves at least reflect her physical reflexes staying sharp even when her composure cracks.

---

One optional mechanical flourish: if your DM's open to it, ask about reflavoring a cantrip or minor racial ability (Shape Water, if available in your ruleset) as literally her fidgeting habit — fixing drips, redirecting spilled water, etc. It'd let her "quirk" show up mechanically, not just narratively.
