# Tavian “Tav” Runewick
**Rock Gnome Artificer — Level 2**  
**Potential future multiclass:** Cleric, much to Tav’s annoyance

## TL;DR

Tav Runewick comes from a comfortable, respected lineage of gnome wizards. His parents, **Aldren and Mirabel Runewick**, were practicing wizards before becoming academics, and his older sister **Elira** successfully followed the family tradition.

Tav was expected to do the same. He entered their prestigious magical college on the traditional arcane track, but switched to its **School of Applied Thaumaturgy and Mechanica** after discovering that he was much more interested in understanding, building, repairing, and applying magic than simply practicing it.

A longtime family friend and master craftsman, **Edric Veyne**, was largely responsible. Edric taught Tav practical craftsmanship as a child, gave him his first battered set of tools, and eventually pointed him toward applied thaumaturgy.

Tav became a traveling artificer, repairman, salvager, and reverse-engineer of ancient magical technology. Ordinary repairs pay the bills; weird things are what interest him. He particularly loves figuring out how ancient civilizations solved practical problems and adapting those solutions to modern life.

His basic philosophy:

> **“Magic isn't mysterious. We just haven't figured out how it works yet.”**

He respects scholarship and reads constantly, but believes knowledge is incomplete until somebody tests it and does something useful with it.

This also makes him almost irresistibly attracted to unexplained mechanisms, artifacts, traps, ruins, and magical phenomena.

At some point before the campaign, Tav encountered an ancient divine object/mechanism/site referred to here as **Rune Thing™**. He determined that part of it wasn't functioning properly.

So, naturally, he fixed it.

Something divine noticed.

# DM Playground — Rune Thing™, the God, and “The Phenomenon”

**Rune Thing™ is deliberately undefined.** Please make it whatever fits the campaign: artifact, shrine, rune, ancient mechanism, piece of divine infrastructure, etc.

Likewise, please choose the deity and decide **before play** what relationship already exists between Tav and that deity. They can converse directly, communicate occasionally, use signs and visions, or the deity can remain completely silent. Just tell me what Tav already knows or has experienced and I'll build that into his RP from session one.

### Phase I — “The gods are so fucking dramatic.”

Tav accepts that gods exist. He simply finds divine communication unnecessarily theatrical.

**If they speak directly:**

> **God:** “Seek what lies beneath the forgotten stone.”  
> **Tav:** “You could just give me an address.”

**If the god uses signs:**

> Seven candles extinguish except the easternmost. Tav relights them twice to establish repeatability.
>
> “Fine. Apparently we're going east. My god has apparently never heard of nouns.”

He isn't disrespectful toward faith or religious people. His irritation is specifically with gods who possess enormous cosmic knowledge yet apparently refuse to communicate efficiently.

### Phase II — “Aw, fuck. Why can *I* do that?”

If it fits the campaign, I'm leaving open an eventual **Artificer/Cleric multiclass**, but I'd prefer not to know when or how it will happen.

A god doing divine things doesn't surprise Tav.

**Tav suddenly doing divine things absolutely does.**

His response will be to investigate his new abilities experimentally, initially referring to them as **“the phenomenon.”**

> A wilted bush suddenly erupts into healthy flowers.
>
> Tav stares at it.
>
> “…aw, fuck.”
>
> *Notebook comes out.*
>
> “Nobody touch the bush.”

Expect harmless experiments on flowers, fruit, insects, furniture, minor scratches, etc. These are RP experiments, not attempts to gain free mechanical effects.

His entire life has taught him that scholarship, observation, experimentation, and engineering can explain how systems work.

Now he gets to find out whether **divinity works that way too.**

# 1. Where does your character feel like they belong?

The **Runewick family home** is Tav's real home: a comfortable old house full of books, magical curiosities, academic correspondence, visiting scholars, and generations of accumulated objects nobody could quite justify throwing away.

His parents, **Professors Aldren and Mirabel Runewick**, began as practicing wizards before entering academia. Tav loves them, despite their lingering belief that their very talented son *could have been a wizard*.

His older sister **Elira** actually did become one, and the two have an affectionate intellectual rivalry.

Tav misses the noise, arguments, enormous library, and his mother's simultaneous ability to critique his methodology and ask whether he's eating properly.

His traveling workshop-wagon has become a second home, although nobody else understands its organizational system.

**Small thing from home:** The Runewicks have a distinctive household tea blend Tav would recognize immediately by smell.

He also still carries several tools from the battered tool roll Edric Veyne gave him when he was young.

---

# 2. What made you into the person you are?

Tav's parents gave him **scholarship**: magical theory, history, rigorous study, and respect for accumulated knowledge.

**Master Edric Veyne**, a longtime family friend and craftsman who built specialized equipment for his parents, gave him **craftsmanship**.

When young Tav once complained that he couldn't figure out what was wrong with something broken, Edric told him:

> “You looked *at* it. Now look at it.”

Edric taught him fabrication, materials, repair, tools, and practical diagnosis. He later gave Tav an old battered tool roll and introduced him to the college's School of Applied Thaumaturgy and Mechanica.

Tav switched programs and found his calling.

His philosophy became:

> **Scholarship tells us what people know. Experimentation tells us whether they're right. Engineering asks what useful thing we can do with it.**

**Strong belief that may be completely wrong:**

> **There is no fundamentally mysterious magic. Everything—including divine magic—operates according to principles that could be understood with sufficient information.**

---

# 3. What makes your character happy?

Tav likes **fixing things**.

His hands are rarely idle. During conversations he may clean a tool, cast Mending on something damaged, sketch a mechanism, or fiddle with a salvaged component.

His real passion is ancient engineering. He salvages forgotten mechanisms, determines what their builders were trying to accomplish, and adapts useful ideas to modern problems.

This produces an unfortunate conversational habit:

> “Actually, they automated something like this in the temples of Azeroth…”

This may become a lengthy explanation whether anyone requested one or not.

Given two free weeks, Tav would spend them in his workshop and emerge with several repaired objects, six unfinished experiments, one completely dismantled ancient artifact, and an unsolicited proposal for automating the town's sewage system.

# 4. Who are the important people in their life?

### Professor Mirabel Runewick — Someone he loves

**Mother.** Warm, formidable, curious, and highly educated.

She recognized Tav's magical talent early and expected him to follow the family wizard tradition. His program switch disappointed her because she imagined they might someday share a profession, not because she considers his work inferior.

She's gradually realized they share the same love of discovery; they simply practice it differently.

She tells colleagues Tav specializes in “practical applications of recovered thaumaturgical technologies.”

Tav says he fixes things.

### Elira Runewick — Someone who pushes him

**Older sister.** Brilliant, disciplined, skeptical, affectionate, and completely immune to Tav's bullshit.

Elira became the accomplished wizard everyone expected Tav to become.

They constantly challenge each other's methods. Tav questions her assumptions; Elira stops Tav from outrunning his evidence.

> **Elira:** “You tested it twice.”  
> **Tav:** “It worked twice.”  
> **Elira:** “That isn't the same thing.”

She's often the first person Tav tells about a strange discovery because he knows she won't dismiss him—or humor him.

### Professor Corvin Drell — Someone he conflicts with

**Former professor.** Brilliant, cautious, meticulous, proud.

Drell believed Tav was too willing to experiment with poorly understood systems. Tav believed Drell sometimes confused caution with understanding.

Annoyingly, both have been proven right.

Drell once stopped Tav from attempting something because previous researchers had already established that it was dangerous.

He was completely correct.

Later, Tav restored an ancient mechanism using an approach Drell insisted couldn't work.

Tav was completely correct.

Neither has allowed the other to forget these incidents.

They secretly respect each other and would prefer nobody point that out.

---

# 5. Why did they become an adventurer?

Tav didn't initially consider himself one.

He became a traveling artificer and repairman. Ordinary work funded his travels, while his reputation increasingly brought him unusual artifacts and problems:

> “We found something strange.”

Those are dangerous words around Tav.

He also began salvaging ruins because ancient civilizations often solved problems modern people had forgotten how to solve. Tav believes forgotten knowledge should be **used**, not simply catalogued.

Why manually operate a dangerous floodgate if a ruined city contains the remains of an automated one?

Eventually his travels brought him to **Rune Thing™**.

It was ancient, divine, poorly understood, and apparently malfunctioning.

Tav fixed it.

Whatever happened afterward pulled him into something larger than his normal traveling circuit.

He continues partly from curiosity and partly from responsibility: if fixing Rune Thing™ caused something, walking away because the consequences are inconvenient would be dishonest.

Also, he desperately wants to know what the damn thing actually did.

---

# 6. What are your character's weaknesses?

Tav's greatest flaw is **curiosity combined with confidence that understanding something makes it manageable**.

He's actually cautious with systems he understands.

Unknown systems are irresistible.

A mysterious mechanism, magical anomaly, ancient ruin, strange trap, or artifact everyone agrees should remain untouched immediately gets his attention.

The most dangerous sentence Tav can utter is:

> **“I think I know how this works.”**

He could break the law to prevent knowledge or an irreplaceable artifact from being destroyed. He could pick a fight over deliberate destruction or dangerous ignorance. He'd risk his life readily for someone endangered by one of his decisions.

Threatening his family—Aldren, Mirabel, Elira, Edric—or people he has come to consider family can overwhelm his otherwise methodical judgment.

**Something he's bad at:** Recognizing when an explanation has become a lecture.

He can understand an ancient mechanism and completely fail to notice that the person who asked about it stopped listening five minutes ago.

---

# 7. What would truly matter to your character?

Tav doesn't need fame, enormous wealth, or recognition as a great hero.

He wants to **leave things working better than he found them**.

If ordinary people live safer or easier lives because he recovered forgotten knowledge and made it useful again, he considers that worthwhile.

A village no longer floods.

A dangerous job becomes safe.

A mysterious mechanism becomes something ordinary people can understand and maintain.

Someone he taught eventually improves one of his own designs.

That's a successful life.

Privately, Tav also wants something simpler: after all his traveling, he wants people and somewhere to come home to.

**What he's afraid he'll never experience:**

That he'll never know when to stop looking for the next problem.

Tav worries that someday he'll discover he spent his entire life learning how everything works without ever learning when to put down his tools and simply enjoy what he already had.
