# Naia Stillwater

**Last updated:** 2026-09-12
**Source(s):** My backstory hurts - Graham.md, Naia Stillwater.md

## Summary
Naia Stillwater, known as "The Leak," is a Water Genasi Monk with a unique backstory and a strong sense of duty. Raised in the Cistern Monastery, she learned to control water through rigorous training. After a traumatic event during a storm, she left the monastery to seek answers about her parents' fate. Naia is driven by a desire to understand the past and prove her belief in the power of discipline.

## Details
Naia is a vibrant and meticulous character, with a serene demeanor that belies her inner strength. She is fiercely loyal to her friends, particularly Coen Ashgrove, and is driven by a deep-seated need to understand the world around her. Her training as a monk has given her a unique perspective on control and discipline, but it has also made her vulnerable to situations where she must let go and embrace chaos.

Naia's character is defined by her ability to fix leaks and her strong belief that stillness is a choice. She is a skilled fighter, able to use her precision and calm under pressure to overcome challenges. However, her reliance on discipline has also made her susceptible to moments of panic and overreaction when faced with genuine danger.

Naia's relationships are complex. She has a deep bond with Coen, who taught her to laugh again after the storm. She also has a rivalry with Sera Voss, a fellow initiate who left the monastery in a dispute over the nature of stillness. Naia is driven by a desire to prove her belief in discipline, but she is also haunted by the unresolved wound of her parents' death.

Naia's character is a testament to the power of discipline and the importance of facing one's fears. She is a complex and multi-dimensional character, with a rich backstory and a strong sense of purpose.

**Combat flavor note:** Her Monk's Focus mechanic (Flurry of Blows, Patient/Focused Patient Defense, Step of the Wind) maps well onto her unresolved discipline-vs-raw-self tension. Framing: strikes/actions that *don't* cost a Focus Point are her trained technique — the stillness she was taught. Spending a Focus Point is her reaching for the thing underneath the training — the same raw, uncontrolled something that let her pull Coen out from under the collapsed beam, which Ithren called "not your training, that was you." She doesn't fully trust that reserve even though it works. Good physical tells for it at the table: the color-ripples under her skin moving faster/brighter, or her usually-even breathing/footwork rhythm (the *tok...tok...tok* she's known for) briefly losing its evenness right as she commits the point.
